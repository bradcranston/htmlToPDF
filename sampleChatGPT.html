Bill: Act Relating to Child Endangerment

Summary: This bill amends existing laws regarding child endangerment by expanding the definition of child endangerment, outlining specific acts that constitute endangerment, and clarifying penalties associated with these offenses.

Key Provisions

  • Definition of Child: The bill defines "child" as any person under eighteen years of age, removing specific references to "minors."
  • Acts Constituting Child Endangerment: It delineates several actions that would qualify as child endangerment, including:
    • Knowingly creating substantial risk to a child's physical, mental, or emotional health.
    • Using unreasonable force or cruelty resulting in bodily harm or intending serious injury.
    • Willfully depriving a child of necessary care, food, clothes, or supervision.
    • Knowingly allowing a child to be present in locations where illegal drugs are manufactured or used.
    • Permitting unsupervised access to a child by a registered sex offender.
    • Abandoning a child who cannot care for themselves.
  • Affirmative Defenses: Provides certain defenses for charges of endangerment, such as apprehension of harm in stopping abuse.
  • Penalties: The bill establishes felony charges for:
    • Child endangerment resulting in death (class felony).
    • Child endangerment resulting in serious injury (class felony).
    • Child endangerment resulting in bodily injury (class felony).
    • Other endangerment not classified under a specific paragraph (aggravated misdemeanor).

Current Law Context

Under current law, persons responsible for a child who commit acts leading to endangerment can be prosecuted. The bill aims to provide more detailed definitions and expand the scope of prosecutable actions.

Conclusion

This bill seeks to enhance child protection laws by imposing stricter penalties for endangerment and clarifying the definitions and actions deemed harmful to children under the law.
